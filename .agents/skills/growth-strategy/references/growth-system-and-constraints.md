# Growth System and Constraints

Use this reference to map where growth is currently limited before selecting tactics or channels.

## Principle

A growth constraint is a condition that materially limits improvement in the primary business outcome within the current scope and horizon. A weak metric is not automatically the constraint, and the system may contain more than one meaningful constraint.

Distinguish:

- **Verified constraint** — evidence shows the condition is materially limiting the outcome.
- **Likely constraint** — evidence points to the condition, but important competing explanations remain.
- **Symptom** — a metric or observed behavior that may result from a deeper cause.
- **Dependency** — work that must be completed before another opportunity can be evaluated or executed.
- **Unknown** — potentially decision-changing but not yet evidenced enough to classify.

## System map

Review only the layers relevant to the business model:

1. **Market and demand** — addressable demand, segment fit, buying situation, category/competitive pressure, seasonality, market access.
2. **Acquisition and distribution** — reach, demand capture/creation, channel coverage, targeting, creative supply, query/product coverage, marginal acquisition economics.
3. **Conversion** — landing/product page, form, checkout, sales process, persuasion and usability friction before the conversion boundary.
4. **Activation / first value** — only when a distinct post-conversion value milestone exists.
5. **Retention / continuation** — repeat, renewal, lapse, churn, recovery, and win-back where repeated value is meaningful.
6. **Monetization** — offer, price, package, payment model, discount architecture, contribution and willingness-to-pay evidence.
7. **Operations and capacity** — inventory, fulfillment, sales bandwidth, implementation capacity, customer support, service delivery, cash flow, compliance, production capacity.
8. **Measurement and learning** — source-of-truth quality, causal evidence, conversion lag, attribution limitations, experiment throughput and reusable learning.

This is a diagnostic map, not a mandatory linear funnel. A business may not have a distinct activation or retention layer; a lead-generation business may have a long sales-stage constraint; a marketplace may have supply and demand constraints simultaneously.

## Constraint diagnosis

For each suspected constraint record:

- observed signal
- source and evidence state
- mechanism hypothesis
- competing explanations
- affected segment/channel/product/cohort
- estimated business consequence or range when defensible
- specialist owner
- evidence needed to confirm or reject
- reversibility and cost of acting before certainty

Prefer a constraint that explains several connected observations over one inferred from a single dashboard metric, but do not force a single root cause when evidence supports multiple independent limits.

## Examples of false constraint diagnosis

- “CTR is low, so creative is the bottleneck.” Low CTR can coexist with an offer, targeting, demand, or measurement problem.
- “ROAS is highest on Brand Search, so budget should move there.” Brand demand capture may not be incrementally scalable.
- “Traffic is down, so SEO is the problem.” Demand, seasonality, ranking, indexing, measurement, and competitive entry are separate hypotheses.
- “Activation is low, so onboarding is broken.” Poor-fit acquisition, technical failure, service delay, or a bad activation definition may be the cause.
- “Churn is high, so send win-back emails.” Price, service failure, failed payment, changed need, poor fit, or lack of repeat need require different responses.

## Dependency ordering

Treat these as possible prerequisite classes, not universal precedence:

- measurement defect before interpreting performance
- product/service truth before making claims
- severe delivery/fulfillment/service defect before acquisition expansion
- offer/pricing ambiguity before aggressive promotion
- capacity shortfall before scaling demand
- activation failure before assuming retention messaging is the fix
- immature retention economics before using predictive lifetime value as a major capital-allocation assumption

## Stop conditions

Stop strategic prioritization and route to the relevant owner when:

- the primary business outcome is undefined
- the source of truth is unreliable enough to reverse the decision
- material economics are missing for a capital-allocation decision
- an operational/legal/product constraint is outside the Marketing OS and must be resolved first
- the request requires current platform behavior that has not passed the platform-currency gate

A valid output can be “constraint not yet identified.” In that case prioritize the smallest evidence-gathering action that can materially change the strategic choice.
