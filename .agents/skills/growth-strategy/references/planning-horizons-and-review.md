# Planning Horizons and Review

Use this reference to turn prioritized opportunities into a sequence that respects business timing, dependencies, learning speed, and capacity.

## Horizon selection

Choose the planning horizon from the decision, not from habit.

Relevant inputs include:

- user-requested horizon
- sales cycle and conversion lag
- retention/renewal maturity
- seasonality and launch dates
- inventory or service capacity
- creative/engineering/operations lead time
- cash-flow constraints
- time needed for a test to become decision-grade

A 30/60/90 plan, quarter, month, sprint, or annual roadmap can be useful when it matches the business need. None is a default best practice.

## Sequencing logic

Sequence initiatives by dependency and decision value, not merely expected launch date.

A useful sequence can include:

1. **Protect** — stop verified leakage or business risk.
2. **Unblock** — fix measurement, capacity, product/service, feed, sales-process, or other prerequisite constraints.
3. **Learn** — resolve uncertainty that determines where material resources should go.
4. **Apply / expand** — use validated local learning within its proven scope.
5. **Scale** — only when the relevant scaling owner confirms readiness and economics.
6. **Review / rebalance** — use mature evidence to continue, hold, change, or retire initiatives.

These labels describe dependency logic, not mandatory phases. Several tracks can run in parallel when they do not contaminate each other's learning or exceed capacity.

## Initiative contract

Every plan item should state:

- decision or outcome it serves
- portfolio role
- specialist owner
- dependency / prerequisite
- evidence state
- exact deliverable or test
- primary business outcome or learning objective
- guardrails
- implementation capacity required
- start condition
- review condition / decision window
- stop, rollback, or retirement condition where relevant
- exact status

Avoid vague tasks such as “improve SEO,” “scale Meta,” “optimize website,” or “work on retention.” Name the decision that the work should resolve.

## Parallel work and contamination

Parallel initiatives are acceptable when:

- implementation capacity exists
- their effects can still be interpreted
- one does not invalidate the other's baseline
- they do not create unacceptable customer or operational load
- the business accepts the measurement tradeoff

When several major changes hit the same outcome at once, state that causal learning is weakened and route experiment design to `$tracking-measurement` when necessary.

## Review triggers

Review the strategy before the nominal calendar date when a material trigger occurs:

- primary business outcome or economics move outside guardrails
- a verified constraint is resolved or disproven
- a new stronger constraint appears
- market, inventory, capacity, pricing, offer, or compliance conditions materially change
- a test reaches its predefined decision window
- source-of-truth data or metric definitions change
- a specialist produces evidence that contradicts a key plan assumption
- the opportunity cost of a current initiative materially rises

A strategy is a versioned decision artifact, not a promise to follow a calendar after its assumptions fail.

## Plan revision

When revising:

1. preserve the prior decision and evidence available at the time
2. record what changed
3. identify which assumption, constraint, or evidence was updated
4. state which initiatives continue, change, pause, or retire
5. preserve valid learning even when the initiative itself is stopped
6. do not rewrite prior rationale with hindsight

## Status discipline

Use exact states such as:

- `draft`
- `proposed`
- `approved`
- `in progress`
- `implemented`
- `observing`
- `validated`
- `contradicted`
- `paused`
- `retired`

Plan approval does not approve every live mutation inside the plan unless the authorization register explicitly covers those actions, limits, and expiry.

## Review output

A plan review should answer:

- What changed in the business outcome?
- Which constraint or assumption changed?
- What did we learn?
- Which priorities remain valid?
- What should stop or be deprioritized?
- What new dependency or opportunity emerged?
- Which specialist decision is required next?
- Has any initiative earned a scaling review?

Do not judge plan quality by percentage of tasks completed. Judge it by whether it focused resources on the best-supported opportunities, produced decision-grade learning, protected downside, and adapted when evidence changed.
