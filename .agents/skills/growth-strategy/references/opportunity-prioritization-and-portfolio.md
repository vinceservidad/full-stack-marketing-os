# Opportunity Prioritization and Portfolio

Use this reference after the growth-system review has produced a bounded set of candidate opportunities.

## What counts as a growth opportunity

A candidate must trace to at least one of:

- a verified defect or avoidable loss
- a diagnosed constraint
- evidence of unrealized demand or conversion/value capture
- a replicated or sufficiently strong local learning ready for broader application
- a material capability gap blocking other decisions
- a high-value uncertainty where a focused experiment can change the strategy

“Try TikTok,” “do more SEO,” “launch email,” or “add AI” are not opportunities until tied to a business problem, audience, mechanism, evidence, and owner.

## Prioritization dimensions

Judge directionally using the strongest evidence available:

- **Commercial impact** — likely effect on the primary business outcome, not just a platform metric.
- **Evidence strength** — verified, observed, reconciled, modeled, inferred, asserted, contradicted, or unknown as defined by the OS.
- **Mechanism confidence** — how well the proposed action explains the observed constraint.
- **Reversibility / downside** — financial, customer, brand, compliance, operational, or learning loss if wrong.
- **Time to learn** — how quickly the initiative can produce decision-grade evidence, accounting for lag and maturation.
- **Cost and capacity** — money, creative, engineering, sales, service, inventory, data, and management attention.
- **Dependency value** — whether the initiative unlocks several later decisions.
- **Opportunity cost** — what must be delayed, reduced, or not done if this is prioritized.

Avoid summing arbitrary 1–10 scores into false precision. If a numerical model is required, expose weights, evidence quality, uncertainty, and sensitivity rather than presenting the score as objective truth.

## Portfolio roles

These labels are optional planning aids, not allocation rules:

### Protect

Protect current business value or remove a verified defect: broken checkout, measurement failure, stock/fulfillment issue, policy/compliance risk, severe retention/service defect, or other downside requiring attention.

### Exploit

Apply or expand an opportunity with enough local evidence to justify further use. “Exploit” does not mean automatically scale spend; paid-media expansion still routes through `$optimization-scaling`.

### Explore

Resolve a strategically important uncertainty through bounded research or an interpretable test. Exploration exists to learn, not to maintain a quota of experiments.

### Build capability

Create a prerequisite capability such as measurement integrity, creative capacity, lifecycle infrastructure, product feed quality, sales process, research coverage, or operational capacity where that capability unlocks later growth.

A portfolio may use none, some, or all four roles. Do not apply a universal percentage to them.

## Priority logic

Prefer, in order only when evidence supports it:

1. material verified defects or downside that threaten the current business outcome
2. blockers that prevent reliable decisions
3. high-confidence opportunities with favorable economics and capacity
4. strategically important learning that can change future allocation
5. speculative opportunities whose cost and downside are acceptable

This is not a rigid ladder. A severe but slow-to-fix defect can coexist with a small reversible growth test; a learning initiative can outrank a modest exploitation opportunity when it unlocks a much larger decision.

## Channel portfolio rules

- Do not diversify merely to have more channels.
- Do not concentrate merely because one channel has the highest attributed ROAS.
- Separate demand capture from demand creation where relevant.
- Evaluate saturation, marginal efficiency, audience overlap, operational capacity, creative requirements, and measurement quality.
- Preserve channel-specific specialist ownership.
- Treat competitor channel presence as context, not evidence that the channel will work for this business.
- When a new channel requires current platform assumptions, use the owning platform skill and `PLATFORM-CURRENCY.md`.

## Explicit non-priorities

Every meaningful strategy should name what is not being prioritized and why. Common valid reasons:

- weaker evidence
- lower expected business impact
- dependency not ready
- insufficient capacity
- unacceptable downside
- long time-to-learn relative to the decision horizon
- opportunity duplicates a stronger initiative
- specialist evidence contradicts the idea

A plan that includes every plausible idea has not made a strategic choice.

## Decision record

For each prioritized initiative record:

- opportunity / problem
- portfolio role
- business outcome affected
- evidence state and source
- mechanism hypothesis
- specialist owner
- dependencies
- expected cost/capacity
- downside/guardrails
- learning or outcome needed
- review/stop condition
- exact state: idea | proposed | approved | in progress | implemented | observing | validated | contradicted | retired

Do not upgrade exact state merely because the plan was approved.
