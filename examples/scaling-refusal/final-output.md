**Hold the proposed budget increase.** The current figures do not establish contribution profit after media, a reliable scaling baseline, or capacity for additional demand. Nothing has been changed in the account.

Meta's 4.2 ROAS is a period-average attributed result. The supplied prior period was 4.4, so it does not support the claim that reported ROAS is the best yet. Neither figure establishes whether increasing budgets will improve the business outcome.

Revenue attributed by Meta fell while spend rose. That is a descriptive comparison, not evidence that the extra £400 caused a loss. A flash sale appeared only in the recent period, and lag and other differences have not been resolved. We cannot use this comparison to estimate marginal contribution or the return from another budget increase.

Meta and Google report a combined £80,200 against Shopify net revenue of £71,200. The £9,000 gap is 12.6% of Shopify net revenue. Overlapping attribution, reporting windows, value definitions and collection problems require separate checks. The gap does not by itself prove a tracking defect or make each platform report unusable.

The cost evidence is also incomplete. If we assume COGS is exactly 45% of net revenue, subtracting that assumed COGS and known Meta spend leaves £25,160. This is an illustrative subtotal, not verified contribution profit after media: Google spend and applicable fulfillment, payment and other variable costs are still missing. Discounts and recorded refunds are already deducted in Shopify net revenue and must not be deducted again.

Three ads have been running since February. That warrants inspecting exposure, response trends, concept concentration and the production pipeline. It does not establish fatigue or show that creative capacity is the binding constraint.
